Lockout/tagout isolates every energy source of a machine so it cannot start, move or release energy while someone works on it. It is required before any work inside guards, on hydraulics, pneumatics, electrical cabinets, belts, couplings or bearings. Only trained and authorised people apply locks; each person working on the machine applies their own personal lock.

## Steps

1. Prepare: identify every energy source of the machine from its manual (electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic, stored mechanical energy, gravity, heat). Tell the operators and the shift leader that the machine will be stopped.
2. Stop the machine with its normal stop sequence (cycle stop, then main stop), never by pulling the main isolator under load.
3. Isolate: open the main electrical isolator and every other isolating device (air supply valve, hydraulic isolation valve).
4. Lock and tag: put your personal padlock and a danger tag with your name, date and reason on each isolating device. Use a multi-lock hasp when several people work.
5. Release stored energy: discharge hydraulic accumulators, bleed air lines, lower or block raised parts (press slides, robot arms), wait for rotating parts to stop and hot parts to cool.
6. Verify zero energy: try to start the machine from its normal controls (then return them to off), check voltage absence with a tested voltage detector, and check that pressure gauges read zero.
7. Work. If you leave the site, your lock stays on; a lock is never removed by someone else except through the site's supervised lock removal procedure.

## Returning the machine to service

1. Remove tools and materials, refit every guard and cover.
2. Check that everyone is clear of the machine and tell the operators.
3. Each person removes their own lock and tag.
4. Restore energy in the reverse order of isolation and run a test cycle at reduced speed while watching for leaks, noise or vibration.
5. Record the work and the test result in the work order.

## Personal protective equipment

Safety shoes and glasses always. Add cut-resistant gloves for sharp parts, heat-resistant gloves for parts above 50 °C, face shield when opening hydraulic lines, and hearing protection above 85 dB(A).
