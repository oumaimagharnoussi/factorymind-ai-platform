> Demo content written for FactoryMind. Illustrative only.

FactoryMind measures vibration velocity in mm/s RMS on the bearing housings. The plant's limits follow the usual ISO 20816 zones for medium-size machines on rigid foundations: below 2.8 mm/s is good, 2.8 to 4.5 mm/s acceptable for long-term operation, the warning limit is 4.5 mm/s and the critical limit 7.1 mm/s. Above 7.1 mm/s damage can occur quickly: the machine should be stopped.

## When a WARNING vibration alert is raised (above 4.5 mm/s)

1. Check the trend on the Live monitoring screen and the failure risk in the AI risk center: a slow rise over hours points to wear (bearings, misalignment), a sudden jump to a mechanical event (loose part, broken blade, impact).
2. Listen and look without opening guards: unusual noise, rubbing, hot bearing housing (compare both sides with an infrared thermometer), loose foundation bolts, damaged coupling guard.
3. Check what changed: recent maintenance, new product or speed, coupling or belt replaced. A rise right after work often means misalignment or a missing balancing weight.
4. Plan an inspection work order within 48 hours. Do not wait for the critical limit.

## When a CRITICAL vibration alert is raised (above 7.1 mm/s)

1. Tell the shift leader and stop the machine at the end of the current cycle, or immediately if there is noise of metal contact, smoke or a smell of burning.
2. Apply lockout/tagout before any inspection.
3. Inspect in this order: foundation and fixing bolts, coupling (wear of elastic elements, alignment), bearings (play, grease condition, discoloration), fan and rotor (dirt build-up, broken blades), belts and pulleys.
4. Replace damaged parts, re-align couplings to within 0.05 mm offset, and re-tighten bolts to the torque of the machine manual.
5. Restart and measure again; the reading should be back below 2.8 mm/s within the first hour.

## Typical causes and signatures

- Unbalance: vibration at 1x running speed, mostly radial, rises with the square of the speed. Clean the rotor or balance it.
- Misalignment: 1x and 2x running speed, high axial vibration, coupling runs hot. Re-align.
- Bearing wear: rising high-frequency noise, temperature of the bearing housing rises with vibration. Re-grease if the grease is dry; replace the bearing if noise stays.
- Looseness: many harmonics, vibration changes when bolts are touched. Re-tighten, check for cracked feet.
